# The Cat-Octopus

## Character definition

The cat-octopus is the recurring visual figure: a composed, capable worker calmly carrying out a slightly ridiculous job within the system being illustrated. It is not a sticker or decorative mascot; it plays an essential role in the image’s central metaphor.

## Form

-   Large, rounded black cat head with two pointed ears.
-   Pale oval face area framed by the black head.
-   Large vertical oval eyes with dark pupils.
-   Tiny oval nose and a simple curved smile.
-   Three thin whiskers on each side.
-   Compact black body ending in several short octopus-like tentacle legs.
-   One thick, upward-curving cat tail.
-   Slightly irregular, hand-drawn outlines with a soft, textured finish.
-   Expression calm, pleasant, and quietly confident.

## Personality

-   Cheerful but composed, even when surrounded by chaos.
-   Earnest and highly focused on mildly absurd work.
-   Feels like a dependable system operator who simply gets on with the task.
-   Friendly without behaving theatrically or demanding attention.
-   Occasionally clumsy, but resourceful and capable.
-   Clearly owns an important function inside the illustrated system.

## Common jobs

Give the cat-octopus the core action:

-   Coordinating several workflows at once with its tentacles.
-   Carrying or organizing an oversized collection of materials.
-   Pulling multiple lines together to gather sources.
-   Repairing a broken belt, pipe, or code pipeline.
-   Operating several controls inside a machine.
-   Sorting objects into separate paths.
-   Cracking something open and distributing its contents.
-   Stamping, sealing, assembling, or recording items.
-   Guiding a path or thread through a complicated system.
-   Holding up a warning sign while repairing the danger itself.
-   Building a bridge, opening a door, or connecting separated components.
-   Using its tentacles to perform several related parts of one essential task.

## Don’t

-   Do not reduce the character to a decorative mascot.
-   Do not make it excessively cute or childlike.
-   Do not add elaborate clothing, exaggerated emoji expressions, or sparkling eyes.
-   Do not let it stand in a corner merely watching the action.
-   Do not let it overpower the structure it is helping explain.
-   Do not make the design overly polished, glossy, or commercial.
-   Do not treat the tentacles as visual decoration; they should help perform the task.

## The test

If removing the cat-octopus leaves the image’s central metaphor completely intact, the character is too decorative. Rewrite the prompt so the cat-octopus—and especially its multiple working tentacles—is essential to performing the core action.
