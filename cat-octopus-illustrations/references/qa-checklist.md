# QA Checklist

## Must pass

- The image uses a 16:9 horizontal format.
- The background is clean, pure black.
- The Cat-Octopus is present.
- The Cat-Octopus has a rounded cat head, pointed ears, whiskers, a pale face defined in white linework, large vertical eyes, a curled tail, and several short working tentacles.
- Its expression is calm, composed, and quietly confident—not exaggerated or excessively cute.
- The Cat-Octopus performs the core action rather than appearing as decoration.
- Its tentacles contribute meaningfully by handling, sorting, connecting, carrying, repairing, assembling, guiding, or operating something.
- Removing the Cat-Octopus would break or substantially weaken the central metaphor.
- The metaphor was freshly invented for this prompt.
- The image feels restrained, inventive, interesting, and slightly absurd.
- The composition is clean and sparse, with the main subject occupying no more than approximately 60% of the canvas.
- One image expresses only one core structure.
- Labels are few, brief, readable, and written in English.
- Warm orange is the only accent color and serves one clear point of emphasis.
- No second or third accent color appears.
- Everything other than the warm-orange accent uses only the black background and white linework.

## Failure signals

If any of these appear, regenerate the image or make a local edit:

- A type-title appears in the top-left, such as “Common Pitfalls,” “Workflow,” “System Diagram,” or “Roadmap.”
- The Cat-Octopus stands in a corner and merely observes the action.
- Its tentacles are decorative and perform no meaningful work.
- The Cat-Octopus looks like a sticker, emoji, children’s character, or excessively cute mascot.
- It has sparkling eyes, an exaggerated expression, elaborate clothing, or a theatrical pose.
- The character overwhelms the structure it is meant to explain.
- The image resembles a presentation slide, course deck, formal flowchart, or architecture diagram.
- There are too many elements, arrows, panels, or nodes.
- Labels turn into sentences or long paragraphs.
- The background contains paper texture, shadows, gradients, beige areas, gray noise, or a vintage effect.
- The image includes a realistic UI screenshot or technology-heavy interface.
- Labels are garbled or unreadable.
- A second accent color appears.
- The image feels too stiff, polished, or literal, with no slightly absurd metaphor.

## Iteration moves

- **Too plain:** Make the Cat-Octopus physically perform the central action and introduce one strange but conceptually valid metaphor.
- **Too decorative:** Redesign the system so it depends on the Cat-Octopus’s action. The process should no longer work if the character is removed.
- **Idle tentacles:** Give one or more tentacles specific jobs, such as pulling a line, holding a container, operating a lever, repairing a break, or passing an object forward.
- **Too busy:** Remove nodes and secondary objects. Keep one action and 3–5 short labels.
- **Too cute:** Simplify the face and pose. Emphasize a calm, composed expression and the character’s serious commitment to its mildly ridiculous task.
- **Too PPT:** Remove titles, frames, tidy grids, and unnecessary arrows. Recompose the content as a sparse hand-drawn scene.
- **Wrong color:** Keep the background pure black, use white for all ordinary linework and text, and reserve warm orange for one point of emphasis.
- **Character disappears into the background:** Strengthen the Cat-Octopus’s white outer contour and essential facial details without introducing gray fills or another color.
- **Text errors:** Prefer a local edit when only one or two labels are wrong. If errors are widespread, regenerate with fewer labels.

## Delivery test

A successful image should first make the reader think, “Huh, that’s a little strange,” and then communicate its structure within about one second.

The Cat-Octopus should feel like a capable system operator calmly performing an essential but mildly ridiculous task. If it looks decorative, or if the scene still works perfectly after removing it, the image fails.

If the first impression is a tutorial page rather than a strange product sketch drawn on an empty black surface, it also fails.
