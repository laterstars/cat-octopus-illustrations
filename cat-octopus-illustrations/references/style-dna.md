# Style DNA

## One sentence

Pure black, minimal, hand-drawn, lots of blackspace, restrained, calm, and slightly absurd, with the feeling of a quick product sketch—a Cat-Octopus drawn in white linework, one warm-orange accent, and a structure that is clear without becoming an instruction manual.

Like someone who has spent years working with AI, products, and design tools quickly sketching one explanatory drawing on a black sheet of paper.

## Must have

-   A 16:9 horizontal concept illustration.
-   A pure black background: no beige, warm gray, paper texture, gradient, shadow, noise, or vintage effect.
-   Mostly white hand-drawn line art: thin, slightly wobbly lines that never look mechanical, vector-perfect, or heavily outlined.
-   The Cat-Octopus appears by default and performs the image’s core action.
-   The Cat-Octopus has a large rounded cat head, pointed ears, whiskers, a pale face suggested through white linework, large vertical eyes, a curled tail, and several short working tentacles.
-   Its expression is calm, friendly, and quietly confident—never exaggerated or overly cute.
-   Its tentacles actively handle, sort, connect, carry, repair, or coordinate the central objects whenever appropriate.
-   Lots of blackspace: the main subject fills roughly 40%–60% of the canvas, while at least 35% remains empty, ideally forming one complete quiet zone.
-   A few short handwritten English labels: at most 5–8, each kept as brief as possible, ideally 1–3 words.
-   One image expresses only one core action, structure, state, or metaphor.
-   Let the structure read naturally; never write the structure type’s name on the image.
-   The Cat-Octopus must be essential to the metaphor. If removing it leaves the idea fully intact, revise the composition so it performs the central action.

## Color

This is a **single-accent duotone**: black plus exactly one accent color.

-   **White** — the Cat-Octopus’s outlines and facial details, surrounding structures, frames, labels, and main objects.
-   **Warm orange** — the single element the eye should follow: the Cat-Octopus’s active tentacle, the primary flow, a key arrow, a critical word, the result, or the moment of emphasis.

Because the Cat-Octopus is naturally black, define it against the black background with a clear white outer contour and sparse interior details. Do not fill its body with gray or introduce another color merely to improve visibility.

Use the accent sparingly and give it only one job per image. Never introduce a second or third color. If tempted to add another color, use orange or leave the element black.

Warm orange is the default accent. If the user requests a different signature color, replace orange with that color everywhere while retaining only one accent.

## Never

-   No Cat-Octopus standing in a corner merely observing the action.
-   No decorative or sticker-like use of the character.
-   No excessively cute, childish, or mascot-like treatment.
-   No elaborate clothing, sparkling eyes, emoji expressions, or theatrical poses.
-   No idle tentacles added only to make the silhouette interesting.
-   No commercial illustration.
-   No PPT infographic.
-   No formal flowchart.
-   No course slide.
-   No cute cartoon poster.
-   No children’s illustration.
-   No complex architecture diagram.
-   No polished flat illustration.
-   No technology-heavy UI.
-   No realistic application screenshot.
-   No complex background, gradient, shadow, or texture.
-   Do not explain every node.
-   No second accent color.
-   No type-title in the top-left corner, such as “Workflow,” “System Diagram,” “Common Pitfalls,” or “Roadmap.”

## Aesthetic direction

Aim for calm, inventive, interesting, clean, and slightly absurd.

The Cat-Octopus should feel like a capable system operator: quietly managing an important but mildly ridiculous task with several working tentacles. Its presence provides dry visual humor without distracting from the structure it explains.

Avoid anything excessively cute, childish, complicated, polished, or stiff.
